One

1 October 2026

Actual last first day of school! This would be 17th grade? At this point I feel like one of those old people that's so over it that they've stopped counting how old they are. I have practically no idea what I've signed up for. My dad and I came and visited campus a few days ago... I'm not sure what I was expecting. First of all, it's fucking cold (not really). Texas heat was one thing but now I HAVE to put on pants and a jacket to go to class and it's only September. This was fine in high school because I was legally obligated to go, but now I'm in 17th fucking grade and I'm paid regardless of whether I show up! In fact, the resulting guilt is what gets me up in the morning.

Anyways, campus. There is a parabolic relationship between the niceness of a college building and its age. Considering that the University of Aberdeen has existed since 1495(!), some buildings here are at peak awesomeness. The Meston Building, however, sits firmly at the very nadir of that curve. Half of it looks okay, but the chemistry department is in the 1970s-era brutalist half. We can talk shit for hours about UT's equally ugly physics building, but at least we had the tallest building on campus. And a telescope on top. And they're constructing a new building for us as I type. Meston, despite having none of PMA's claims to fame, is apparently protected in some way, so we're stuck here forever. Our office has a window... into another room. But I think that room might have a real window!

Some heartwarming news: one day of school, and I 100% know that I've made the right decision. (Whatever Stockholm syndrome I've developed that makes me feel less anxious when I'm in school might need to be addressed later, though.) Dr. Seymour (pictured above) is actually angel perfect. Like no hate to all the other professors I've had, but this one might be the GOAT. He is nice and is also cool, which rarely happens! Maybe because I was in physics before—truly unparalleled in the chud department. But like since when did they make professors that also used to play grunge bass? I want to be him when I grow up (except I still want to be famous.)

The chemistry department does a tea-time social every Thursday morning. I'm so glad that my first experience here is so quintessentially British. I don't even like tea but I'm tickled regardless. And everyone is so enthused by the States here! My postdoc, Jon, has only visited Nashville for some reason (there's a national lab there, I think). I'd be disappointed if I was him. Like my cousin, who spent a whole month and a half in America and only got to see the sights of Renton, Washington. Although I imagine that’s exactly what the locals think of me, given that Aberdeen is the only place in Europe I’ve ever been to.
The other two grad students I've met, Megha and Muhammad, are both Malayali, which seems statistically improbable. Good for them! (I haven't met Su yet but she is [I think] Thai.) Jon is the only other British person in our group, and he vehemently insisted that field hockey (which he played) was the real hockey. This country is not real!
